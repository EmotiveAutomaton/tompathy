## 1. The theory in one page

**Inherited.** Six needs, each a target with a band: Knowledge, Nest, Belonging, visibly respectable, visibly compassionate, and the body (Warmth, Food, Sleep). The two visible needs are read as rank among the people around a person. Knowledge and the body are read against the world. Nine channels, which are ready-made answers: Panksepp's seven (SEEKING, RAGE, FEAR, LUST, CARE, PANIC/GRIEF, PLAY) plus DISGUST and SHAME. Pride, guilt and embarrassment are labels, not channels.

**What fires a channel.** A trigger is a change on a need. An appraisal is the set of such changes across the needs. For each need it carries which way the need moved, by whose doing, and, provisionally, whether it has happened or is still to come. It has no other name.

| What just moved, on any need | What can be done | Channel |
|---|---|---|
| a sudden drop | I can get it back by pushing | RAGE |
| a drop coming, fast | get clear of it | FEAR |
| a drop that has already happened | only someone else can undo it | PANIC/GRIEF |
| a drop in how I'm seen, below what I'd forecast | it can't be contested | SHAME |
| a gap that looks closable | look, try, approach | SEEKING |
| nothing loud, and surprise on offer | take it on, safely | PLAY |
| someone else's drop | I can fix it for them | CARE |
| something that would spoil what I hold | keep it out | DISGUST |
| an opening to reproduce | close the distance | LUST |

A channel has three ways in: a gap, leftover, or a summons from a fluency.

**Learned: the Toolbox.** All of it is expertise. A fluency is something a person does cheaply, again and again. It has a readiness, a price, and an effect (what it lights up or summons). Readiness has two inputs, both his: which of its cues are present, and how much it has been used. Four kinds sit on one scale, what a person would trade each for: habit, heuristic, value, identity. A fluency held in attention is a schema. A prompt is a schema handed in from outside. A move that is absent where its trigger fit, from a maker who surely owns it, counts as a refusal.

**Attention** is schema swapping: one beam that hops, on inner things as on outer ones. Memory search is a walk along associations that drifts unsteered or is steered by a swapped-in schema. Listening has two routes: a cheap one by association and a costly one that runs the listener's own generation. Meaning is measured against what would have been said.

**The outer loop** has one aim, leave nothing unresolved. It reads Stress and can only move attention and rearrange fluencies. **Stress** is what is unresolved now: free, bound by a channel, or resolved once a plan has it. What is left over is colored: it carries into the next thing, where a model's sampling noise is white. **Capital** is the range of futures still within reach. It is not a need.

**People differ** in sensitivity to drops (his Neuroticism; he expects one dial and wants the data to decide) and in where the targets sit. Extraversion is a level: happier in general.

- **E13. The pause remainder marks a swap** (his idea this round). Take the residual of log pause from D33's frozen joint model. Per writer, does a large residual predict a deletion within the next few words, and a continuation that is more surprising under her own history? Sentence and punctuation boundaries are already terms in the fit; test on words away from them as well. D33's within-writer permutation banks give the floor. If it holds, the engine has a marker of attention inside a typing log, which is what its attention row has been missing.
- **E12a. Own against donor, as prediction.** D33's report says a difference of coefficients "is not a predictive-model comparison". Run the predictive one: held-out fit of log pause with own-history surprise against donor-history surprise on the same base, folds by session. This is the first rung of his two readers. If own never beats donor here, the split has no footing in typing at these history sizes and its test waits for makers with long histories.
- **S21. The readiness curve.** The odds she opens with a given line, cue held fixed (colour and the opponent's first move), against how many times she has played it and how long since she last did. Then the same for word reuse in the typing logs. Memory in general follows both: "[the probability that a memory will be needed also shows reliable relationships to frequency, recency, and pattern of prior exposures](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.1991.tb00174.x)". His readiness has two inputs, cues and use. The thread's wording added how lately. This card says whether how lately is a third input, and it gives the engine a rule for weighting a maker's own history.
- **S14a. Cadence at sentence grain.** The Court transcripts carry "[the timestamp (as listed in Oyez) of when each sentence in the text starts](https://convokit.cornell.edu/documentation/supreme.html)" and ends, with no audio. Per speaker: words per second in a sentence against the sentence's mean surprise under counts fitted to her own earlier arguments and under the field's. Word-level timing in speech waits for CANDOR on the Spark.

### Set 5. Built worlds: the four settings (Ghost)

- **G9. Four settings** (**new**). Give a maker four parameters: how far the incoming outweighs the held schema; the temperature of the swap, apart from the temperature of the walk; a confidence signal that bursts when the chain's confidence rises and whose level sets how hard the maker works; and the weight of unresolved Stress on the plan in hand. - **G10. Why a maker stays** (**new**). Two makers that both wait longer than baseline: one because cost weighs less, one because it believes more strongly that waiting pays. Vary the odds that waiting pays. Published animal work has the staying effect at high odds "[but not with 50 or 25% reward probabilities](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-018-04496-y)"; a study in people found "[a specific reduction of effort cost, and not by any change in the weight of monetary incentives](https://doi.org/10.7554/elife.17282)". How many observations separate the two makers, and is the low-odds condition required?
