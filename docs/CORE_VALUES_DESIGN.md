# Core values: visual directions

14 October 2026 · Contour currents selected by the creator and implemented. Other directions below remain reference alternatives.

The list should remain quiet enough to read while the article stays primary. The
most useful place for richer imagery is the opened card. Confidence already has
two encodings (weight and the arrow); category decoration should not introduce
another apparent score.

## 1. Contour currents — selected

Both marks now use rounded, tapered curves drawn specifically for this interface.
A drive gathers into a rounded lobe around one rising current; a constructed value
has three overlapping contours around a continuous middle line. They share the
hierarchy's bulb-and-tip silhouette, the attention panel's green ink, and the
preferred-state curve's flowing center line. The same marks repeat at the start
of the card title. No additional line is drawn between the brain and the graph,
which could incorrectly imply a measured anatomical-to-preference connection.

This connects the three panels without drawing another brain behind the list.
Use one accent color and at most two faint contour strokes. The contour should
be decorative, not look like an additional measured connection. Avoid continuous
animation; a brief reveal when opening a card is enough.

**Tradeoff:** restrained enough to preserve readability, but the icon shapes need
to remain distinguishable at 14–16 pixels. Test them at actual size before using
more elaborate curves.

## 2. Living strata

A drive is a seed-shaped ember. A constructed value is an asymmetric set of
growth rings around that seed. In the card, those layers straighten into the
lifetime curve, suggesting patterns accumulated through experience. Warm paper,
muted green and a small amber accent would make this feel more organic.

**Tradeoff:** this is the strongest history metaphor, but rings can imply age,
strength or an evidence count. Keep their number fixed and avoid changing them
with confidence. Elaborate grain or textures would make the tiny list noisy.

## 3. Neural atlas

Use the left-facing head consistently, with a quiet cortical outline and a few
highlighted network nodes. The list keeps its category icons; only the card
reveals the anatomical picture. Constructed values instead show a diffuse web
of overlapping contours, without claiming that a particular value has a known
brain location.

**Tradeoff:** strongest connection to the main identity and to the user's request
for channels, but also the greatest risk of looking more scientifically precise
than the account supports. The implemented drive sketches explore this direction
already. Keep the geometry clearly schematic and the labels available on hover.

## Basis and limits of the network sketches

These are distributed proposed systems, not seven isolated brain spots. Region
sets follow Panksepp's own teaching table in the [Washington State University
lecture](https://s3.wp.wsu.edu/uploads/sites/1135/2016/02/Panksepp.pdf). The
[2010 primary paper](https://aztrauma.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/01/Dialogues-Panksepp-2010.pdf)
provides the broader account of affective systems and subcortical organization.

The SVG projects selected regions into an approximate left-facing sectional
view. Highlighted nodes are a visual index, not a registered atlas. Connecting
strokes group the regions; their order is not a claim about anatomical pathways,
causal direction or measured activity in the creator. At this size, the useful
signal is cortical versus deeper network involvement, not anatomical precision.
More exact medical illustration would need a licensed atlas and expert review.

The lifetime curve, uncertainty tube and evidence vectors are the project's
proposed preference visualization. Combining these with constructed values does
not establish a unified Panksepp–Barrett taxonomy. No backend inference or brain
measurement is connected.
