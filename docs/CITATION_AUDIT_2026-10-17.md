## Remaining claim-scope issues, in order of impact

1. **Core values, the adrenaline/creativity sentence (local reference 45;
   displayed 90).** The cited
   [stress experiment](https://www.jneurosci.org/content/29/22/7191)
   concerns a shift toward habitual rather than goal-directed control. It does
   not establish that adrenaline expands awareness of possible solutions or
   directly validates the proposed transition-map mechanism. The sentence gives
   that experiment a broader explanatory role than its tested outcome supports.
2. **Expertise, the facial-feedback and mirror-neuron passage.** The
   [Many Smiles study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-022-01458-9)
   supports a limited facial-feedback effect involving happiness, voluntary
   facial action and mimicry; its pen-in-mouth findings were less conclusive.
   It does not by itself establish a fear-face heart-rate effect or the proposed
   mirror-neuron simulation mechanism. Those links remain the author's account.
3. **The doctor's caption.** The
   [colonoscopy study](https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/id/eprint/191005/)
   reports adenoma detection changing from 28.4% to 22.4%: six percentage points,
   approximately 21% relative. It is an observational result for a specific
   detection measure, not a 20% fall in general medical accuracy or a test of
   whether users recognized AI. The author's expected skill-decay comparison
   remains uncited by explicit instruction; no comparable source was established.
4. **Expertise, declining human AI detection.** The cited detection studies have
   different samples and tasks. The
   [ACL study of frequent LLM-writing users](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.267/)
   supports the qualification about experienced users in its tested English
   nonfiction setting. It does not itself establish a population-wide decline
   over time. Treat the broader trend and causal interpretation as separate
   from those individual study results.

These are the remaining citation-to-claim gaps, not another request to rewrite
