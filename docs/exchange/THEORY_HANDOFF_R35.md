### Trunk PA: reading a person (the target)

**PA1. The person, scaled up, with a learning curve (claims P3, P2; the engine; phase 0, then the week).**
- Data: the blog corpus on local disk. Your across-days record counts 5,428 authors with at least 20 dated posts on at least five dates; card TP43 used 2,000 of them and up to 40 openings each.
- Ask: all qualifying authors. Then the same matching step with 5, 10, 20 and 40 posts per half, and with whole posts in place of openings where the reader's speed allows.
- Registered (the theory's): the step that need tags add to style and topic grows with the number of posts read, and does not vanish with whole posts.
- Why first: it is register row G1, how many page readings until a maker's settings are known, never run, and it is the engine's own target as a curve.
- Limit to carry, as TP43 filed: "Repeated circumstances and topics beyond the 300 measured words can still account for similarity."

**PA2. Which needs carry her (claim P2; line E5 of the catalog; the engine; phase 0).**
- Ask: the matching step with each need's tags left out in turn (Competence, Meaning, Belonging's family and contact, the tag named belonging apart, Compassion, Respect, the body tags). Then her six need scores as a profile: is the need that reads highest for her in one half the highest in the other, more often than chance?
- Registered (the theory's): Respect's and the body's tags carry the most; the tag named belonging adds nothing by itself; her top need repeats above chance.
- This is the first table of a person's weights on the needs. Your pending feature-subset pass covers part of it.

**PA3. Her own version of a word, in free text (claim P3; the engine; phase 0).**

**PA4. The person in diaries, with a trait in hand (claims S3, P4; Sounding Line; phase 0).**

**PA5. Two reports of one moment (claims P2, T8; Sounding Line; phase 0, if the codebooks allow).**
- Data: the teaching diary, with a teacher's and a mentor's assessment of the same occasion, and the couples' diary, with both partners reporting their own feelings at the same moments.
- Ask of the first: how much of the two raters' agreement is about the occasion and how much is each rater's usual level. Ask of the second: which of her feelings move with his at the same moment, with each person's own average taken out.

### Trunk VO: voice (phase 1)

**VO1. The kinds of laughter, by sound (claims T7, LP5).**
- Data: the meeting recordings, the sixty conversation recordings matched to transcripts, and the sealed conversation set under its terms, which allow modelling of voice and forbid voice prints.
- Ask: cut every marked laugh. Describe each by the handful of measures used in print (voiced or not, pitch, length, number of bursts). Group them without labels. Then test the groups against things the grouping never saw: whether the laugher was the speaker, whether others joined, what the person laughed at did in the next minute, and in the sealed set the partner's later ratings.
- Registered (his): a few kinds that sound different; in meetings the reward kind is by far the commonest and the mocking kind is rare and sits close to the real thing. Registered (in print): spontaneous laughs are less voiced and higher.
- This is the measure of play that isn't mood.

**VO2. PLAY on that measure (claim T7).**
