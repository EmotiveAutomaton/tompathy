## 4. The legs for the next 24 hours, ranked

The first three are one ladder on two kinds of data. Split each person's material into two halves. Ask which of the model's numbers come out the same in both halves, then whether her first-half profile picks her second half out of the crowd, and whether the model's numbers add to plain averages or to writing style.

**1. A writer's own version of a word (claims P3 and P2; the engine; saved outputs only).**
- Data: the chat-situations source, 24,850 situations by 810 writers, each written to one of 32 assigned feeling words, with the reader outputs that queue Q6 is saving.
- For each situation take the nine channel outputs, the need-tag scores (Belonging and Compassion both separate and merged, as he asked) and the worry lift as departures from the mean of the same word over all other writers.
- Stability: for each quantity, the correlation across writers between her two halves, with a writer-resampled interval.
- Picking her out: rank every writer's second half by closeness to her first half. Report top-one and top-ten against chance for a style baseline alone (length, function words, punctuation), the channel departures, the need-tag departures, and each added to the baseline.
- Registered by the theory: the need-tag departures are stable and add to the style baseline.
- Why first: it is leg D7 of the 19 November handoff (his concept vector: a writer's offset for a word, stable across the halves of her text) in a form that needs no new scoring, and register card R6, never run. It runs on data that is already scored, and it gives the first estimate per person of the weight on each need (line E5 of the equation catalog). Card TP32 showed that a stable trait need not identify; this asks the same of the model's own quantities.
- Limit to carry: a stable profile within an assigned word can be a writer's circumstances and not her weights.

**2. A person's own slopes in the diaries (claims P4 and P2; Sounding Line).**
- Data: every admitted diary with enough prompts per person for two halves.
- Three sets of numbers per person and half: her mean on each feeling item; her spread on each; her own within-person slope on each row that cleared or leaned in that diary (criticism to anger and to shame; argument to anger over fear; demanding thought to curiosity; stress to playfulness; a health or money stressor to fear and worry), shrunk toward the group.
- Stability of each slope across halves, then picking her out with the three sets added one at a time.
- Registered by the theory: at least the criticism slopes are stable, and slopes add to means and spreads.
- What it moves: a stable slope is that channel's steepness for that person (line E2), which is claim P4 measured.
